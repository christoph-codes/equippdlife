# Isaiah 6: Holy, Cleansed, and Sent

Isaiah 6 records one of the most powerful moments in Scripture, where Isaiah encounters the holiness of God, is confronted by his own sin, and is commissioned for service. This chapter reveals that a true vision of God always leads to humility, cleansing, and obedience. It also reminds us that even when God’s message includes judgment, His faithfulness preserves hope through a remnant.

## Introduction

Isaiah 6 presents a clear picture of God’s holiness and the proper response to it. Isaiah is given a vision that transforms his understanding of who God is and who he is in comparison. This chapter emphasizes the necessity of reverence, repentance, and readiness to serve.

Responding rightly to God’s holiness prepares us to fulfill His purposes, even when obedience leads into difficult or uncomfortable territory.

## Isaiah’s Vision of the Lord

**Isaiah 6:1-4**  
Isaiah sees the Lord seated on a high and exalted throne, with the train of His robe filling the temple. Surrounding Him are seraphim proclaiming, “Holy, holy, holy is the Lord Almighty; the whole earth is full of His glory.”

This vision highlights God’s majesty, sovereignty, and absolute holiness. The foundations of the temple shake, and the space fills with smoke, emphasizing the weight and power of God’s presence.

God’s holiness should inspire awe and reverence, leading us to worship Him with humility rather than familiarity.

How can we cultivate a deeper sense of reverence for God’s holiness in our daily worship and relationship with Him?

## Cleansing and Commission

**Isaiah 6:5-8**  
Confronted with God’s holiness, Isaiah becomes acutely aware of his own sinfulness. He cries out in despair, recognizing that he is unclean and unworthy to stand in God’s presence.

A seraphim takes a live coal from the altar and touches Isaiah’s lips, symbolizing cleansing and forgiveness. After Isaiah is purified, God asks, “Whom shall I send?” Isaiah responds with willing obedience, saying, “Here am I. Send me!”

This moment shows that God does not call the qualified; He qualifies the called. Cleansing always precedes commissioning.

God equips those He calls, but our responsibility is to respond with humility and readiness.

How can we prepare ourselves to be willing and ready to respond when God calls us to serve?

## The Message of Judgment and Hope

**Isaiah 6:9-13**  
God gives Isaiah a difficult message to deliver. The people will hear but not understand, see but not perceive. Their hearts will be hardened, and judgment will follow. Yet even in this severe message, God reveals hope.

Though destruction will come, a remnant will remain. This faithful group is described as a holy seed, preserved by God for future restoration. Judgment does not erase God’s promises.

This passage reveals both the seriousness of rejecting God and the faithfulness of God to preserve His people.

Even in seasons of discipline and difficulty, God’s grace sustains hope.

How can we remain faithful and hopeful during challenging times, trusting God to preserve and restore what belongs to Him?

## Closing Reflection

Stand in awe of God’s holiness, allow Him to cleanse your heart, and be willing to say yes when He calls. God still works through humble, obedient people to accomplish His purposes, even when the path ahead is difficult.
