# Introduction to Isaiah
**Date:** December 11, 2025

**Scripture Reading:** Isaiah 6:1-8

> *"In the year that King Uzziah died, I saw the Lord, high and exalted, seated on a throne; and the train of his robe filled the temple. Above him were seraphim, each with six wings: With two wings they covered their faces, with two they covered their feet, and with two they were flying. And they were calling to one another: 'Holy, holy, holy is the LORD Almighty; the whole earth is full of his glory.'"*

## Overview of Isaiah

The book of Isaiah stands as one of the most profound and comprehensive prophetic books in the Old Testament. Written by the prophet Isaiah during the 8th century BC, it spans the reigns of several kings of Judah and addresses both immediate concerns of the nation and far-reaching prophecies about the Messiah and the end times.

Isaiah ministered during a tumultuous time in Israel's history. The northern kingdom of Israel fell to Assyria in 722 BC, and Judah faced constant threats from surrounding nations. Yet through it all, Isaiah proclaimed God's message of judgment, hope, and redemption.

## Key Themes

### God's Holiness
Isaiah's vision of God in the temple (Isaiah 6) establishes the central theme of God's holiness. The threefold "Holy, holy, holy" emphasizes the absolute purity and otherness of God. This vision transformed Isaiah and became the foundation of his prophetic ministry.

### Judgment and Salvation
Isaiah prophesies both judgment for sin and salvation through God's grace. He warns of consequences for rebellion but also promises restoration and redemption for those who turn back to God.

### The Coming Messiah
Isaiah contains some of the most detailed Messianic prophecies in the Old Testament. From the virgin birth (Isaiah 7:14) to the suffering servant (Isaiah 53), Isaiah points forward to Jesus Christ with remarkable clarity.

## Isaiah's Call

When Isaiah saw the Lord high and exalted, his first response was to recognize his own sinfulness: "Woe to me! I am ruined! For I am a man of unclean lips" (Isaiah 6:5). This honest acknowledgment of his unworthiness opened the door for God's cleansing and commissioning.

After being cleansed by the coal from the altar, Isaiah heard God's call: "Whom shall I send? And who will go for us?" Isaiah's response is one of the most famous in Scripture: "Here am I. Send me!" (Isaiah 6:8).

## Application for Today

As we study Isaiah, we're invited to:

1. **Encounter God's holiness** - Like Isaiah, we must first recognize who God is before we can understand who we are and what He's calling us to do.

2. **Respond to His call** - God is still asking, "Whom shall I send?" Are we ready to say, "Here am I. Send me"?

3. **Trust His promises** - Even in difficult times, God's word stands firm. His promises of redemption and restoration are sure.

4. **Look forward to Christ** - Isaiah points us to Jesus, the ultimate fulfillment of God's plan of salvation.

## Looking Ahead

In the coming studies, we'll explore key passages from Isaiah, examining both the historical context and the timeless truths that apply to our lives today. We'll see how God's faithfulness to Israel demonstrates His faithfulness to us, and how the prophecies about the Messiah deepen our understanding of Jesus Christ.

## Discussion Questions

1. What does Isaiah's vision of God's holiness teach us about worship?
2. How does recognizing our own sinfulness prepare us for God's call?
3. In what ways do you see Isaiah's prophecies fulfilled in Jesus Christ?
4. What does it mean for you personally to say "Here am I. Send me" to God today?
