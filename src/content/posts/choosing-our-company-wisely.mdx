# Choosing Our Company Wisely  
**Date:** February 9, 2026

**Scripture Reading:** Psalms 26:4

> “I do not spend time with liars or go along with hypocrites.”

David speaks with clarity about the kind of influence he allows into his life. His words reflect intentionality, not isolation. He understands that character is shaped over time by the voices we listen to and the environments we choose to remain in. Discernment is not about judgment; it is about guarding the heart.

This verse reminds us that proximity influences perspective. Spending time with deceitful or dishonest influences slowly normalizes what should never feel acceptable. David chooses integrity by distancing himself from attitudes and behaviors that would pull his heart away from God. His decision reflects a desire to live transparently before the Lord.

Psalms 26 does not suggest withdrawing from the world entirely, but it does call for wisdom. Faithfulness sometimes requires saying no to certain influences so that devotion to God remains clear and undivided. Choosing integrity is often a quiet decision made long before temptation ever appears.

As we move through this season, Psalms 26:4 invites us to reflect honestly on our influences. Who are we allowing to shape our thinking, priorities, and values? Walking closely with God often means choosing relationships and environments that encourage truth, humility, and faithfulness.

## Main Takeaways  
- Our influences shape our character over time.  
- Discernment protects integrity and devotion to God.  
- Choosing wisely often requires intentional boundaries.  

## Cross-Referencing Scriptures  
- **1 Corinthians 15:33:** “Bad company corrupts good character.”  
- **Proverbs 13:20:** “Walk with the wise and become wise…”  
- **Psalms 1:1:** “Blessed is the one who does not walk in step with the wicked…”  

Examine your influences today with honesty. Ask God for wisdom to choose relationships and environments that strengthen your faith, guard your heart, and help you walk with integrity before Him.
