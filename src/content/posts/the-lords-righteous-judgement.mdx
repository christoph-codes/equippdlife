# The Lord’s Righteous Judgment  
**Date:** November 30, 2025  

**Scripture Reading:** Isaiah 34:1-2

> *“Come near, you nations, and listen; pay attention, you peoples! Let the earth hear, and all that is in it, the world, and all that comes out of it! The Lord is angry with all nations; His wrath is on all their armies. He will totally destroy them, He will give them over to slaughter.”*

Isaiah issues a call that reaches beyond Israel — a summons to the entire world. God invites every nation to listen because His judgment is not random or impulsive; it is the righteous response of a holy God to the sin, corruption, and violence taking place in the earth. His anger is not like human anger. It is perfectly just, perfectly measured, and perfectly aligned with His character.

These verses remind us that nothing escapes God’s attention. Every injustice, every act of violence, every moment of rebellion — all of it is seen by the Lord. His silence never means indifference. His patience never means approval. And His timing never means He has forgotten. When God judges, He does so from a place of complete knowledge and perfect holiness. Justice is not merely something He does; it is part of who He is.

For believers, this truth carries weight but also comfort. It reminds us of the seriousness of sin, the reality of God’s holiness, and the depth of His righteousness. Yet at the same time, it leads us to gratitude, because in Christ we are not objects of wrath but recipients of mercy. Jesus took on the judgment we deserved so we could stand before God forgiven, covered, and made new.

## Main Takeaways  
- God’s judgment is rooted in His holiness and perfect justice, not human emotion.  
- The Lord sees and will address all evil, even when it appears unchecked.  
- Because of Christ, believers stand under God’s mercy, not His wrath.  

## Cross-Referencing Scriptures  
- **Nahum 1:3:** “The Lord is slow to anger but great in power…”  
- **Romans 1:18:** “The wrath of God is revealed against all ungodliness…”  
- **2 Corinthians 5:21:** “He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us…”  

Let Isaiah’s words lead you into reverence for God’s holiness and gratitude for the mercy you have in Christ. Justice belongs to the Lord — and so does redemption.
